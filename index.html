Plan the trip, or just pick a city

Five questions — when, how long, who with, what you like — and Narro drafts a day-by-day itinerary around the real forecast. In a hurry, open a city and take a single walk.

Read the stops, then set off

You see what the walk is made of before anything else — real places, with the stories that go with them. The map appears when you press start, not while you are still deciding.

It walks with you

Turn by turn down real streets, counting the metres to the next stop, and each story starts itself the moment you arrive. Not there yet? Simulate the walk.

What it actually does

No pre-recorded tours, no meeting point, and nothing to install

Real places, real coordinates

Every stop is somewhere you can stand: found around you, with the opening lines of its own story read out when you get there.

Turn by turn, on foot

The route follows pavements and crossings rather than a straight line, and the next instruction counts down the metres as you walk it.

Each stop starts itself

Arrive and the story begins — no pressing play at every corner. The trigger widens when your signal is poor, so nothing is missed in a narrow street.

Planned around the forecast

The trip planner reads a real 16-day forecast. Rainy afternoons move indoors, and the days rearrange themselves rather than pretending it is sunny.

Any city you name

Twelve cities are written up in the catalogue. Type any other and the guide finds what is worth seeing there — the walk is built the same way.

Try it from your desk

Not in the city yet? Simulate the walk and it plays out on the map, or click where you are standing. Nothing is hidden behind being there.

Your next walk has a voice

Nothing to download, no account. Answer five questions, or just open a city.
